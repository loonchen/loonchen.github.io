About

I study efficient and reliable networked systems, with a focus on Sustainable Space AI, Efficient AI Infrastructure, and Data-Centric & Embodied AI. My research connects system design with the practical constraints of communication, computation, energy, and physical execution.

I previously worked as a Senior Research Scientist at ByteDance. I conducted postdoctoral research at Simon Fraser University with Prof. Jiangchuan Liu and at The University of Hong Kong with Prof. Edith C. H. Ngai. I received my B.Eng. in Software Engineering in June 2016 and my Ph.D. in Software Engineering in March 2022, both from Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

I collaborate closely with industry partners, grounding my research in production networks, space-system experiments, and industrial robotics. These collaborations support prototype development, evaluation under realistic conditions, and practical deployment.

Sustainable Space AI

My research on Space AI builds on earlier work in satellite networking and space-terrestrial systems. It follows the path from moving data to sustaining computation in orbit: how inference data travel between satellites, how uplink and downlink transfers can be optimized, and where inference should take place. I extend this work to Space AI architectures supported by energy transfer, including radiation-aware AI services, energy-transfer scheduling, and dedicated laser-charging constellations. Across these efforts, I focus on infrastructure design and service models that enable Space AI to operate reliably under microgravity, limited connectivity, and constrained power.

I evaluate these ideas using a real Starlink testbed and experimental platforms provided through industry collaboration. With Liangjian Xingtong (量见星通), I work with optical cameras and laser terminals, alongside facilities for microgravity and vacuum experiments. Xingce Weilai (星测未来) provides in-orbit radiation data, connecting system design and evaluation to measurements from operating satellites.

Efficient AI Infrastructure

My research examines key components of sustainable AI infrastructure: measuring workloads in large AI data centers and using those measurements to inform computation scheduling while meeting application QoS requirements and responding to the needs of the power grid. At a broader level, I aim to make computing demand a flexible grid resource that can adapt as power-system conditions change. This work builds on my research in production networking. ByteSketch, the data structure I developed, is integrated into FlowLog, a system that has operated continuously in ByteDance’s production environment and supports traffic monitoring across thousands of P4 switches.

I have long-standing collaborations with ByteDance, VNET Group (世纪互联), China Datang (中国大唐), and State Grid (国家电网) that connect this research to large-scale computing and energy infrastructure. Through these collaborations, I have managed a fleet of several hundred AI accelerators. I am also investigating the systems foundations of token factories: how large accelerator fleets can be operated as efficient, reliable, and grid-aware production systems for generating AI tokens.

Data-Centric & Embodied AI

I lead the development of embodied-AI foundation models that serve as a shared intelligence layer across diverse robots and industrial equipment. The stack combines multimodal field data and model adaptation through Jiangxing Intelligence’s AutoEdge with world-model-based simulation and pretraining through AutoWorld. The foundation model supports perception, cross-embodiment reasoning, and long-horizon task planning, while an execution harness connects those capabilities to industrial knowledge, operating procedures, and tools, and closes the loop by verifying outcomes in the physical world.

My collaborations with Jiangxing Intelligence (江行智能) and Unitree Robotics (宇树科技) connect this research to real robots and industrial deployments. The foundation models I have developed are now deployed at thousands of real-world industrial sites, providing a large-scale testbed for studying reliable closed-loop operation over time.
